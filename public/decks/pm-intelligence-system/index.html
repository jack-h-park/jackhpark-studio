Why I built this

The problem
  • The problem — Signal overload, no leverage — more relevant signal than I can process; the implication rarely gets extracted, and what I note disappears.
  • Inconsistent decisions under pressure — calls drift with mood/energy as scope grows, exactly when stakes rise.
  • Most AI tools attack the surrounding work (gather/format/file) and leave the judgment architecture to chance.
The response
  • Built toward: philosophy externalized ("more me than me") · knowledge in an AI-leverageable form (Markdown/Obsidian, not a bespoke DB) · an agent that keeps learning my know-how.
  • The bet: operate like a team of specialized junior PMs running on AI — a practical test of whether one PM can do a team's work this way, not just in demos.

Outcomes

The depth ladder works as designed
70% of signals resolve at or before S2; only a small share runs all seven stages. Volume is capped on purpose (Gate 0 holds at 10 pending, 5/day per product), so this is triage, not scarcity.
Depth, not model tier, drives cost
A full decide run costs ~6× an archive. The agent fleet around the pipeline runs on subscription-included calls, so its marginal cost is zero.
Sensing recall, same feeds, before → after
Accepted items doubled, and the candidates the funnel used to burn now stay eligible. Recall is covered by a regression test.
Determinism
The model scores at temperature=0, then code decides: composite and routing are arithmetic, so identical inputs route identically across runs and models.

Design thesis — Consistency · Evolvability

Two quality attributes drove every choice — decisions were drifting (consistency), and I keep rebuilding parts of this (evolvability). Accuracy and speed matter; neither was what was failing. Five principles serve them:

01
Determinism where it counts — two layers: every signal runs the same stages, and within them the model scores while code decides.
02
Human-in-the-loop by design — every gate is where PM judgment decides what happens next.
03
Specialized over autonomous — agents are about specialization, not just automation.
04
Externalized, versioned reasoning — identity, prompts, rubrics as files you can read, diff, roll back.
05
Closed-loop learning — accumulated decisions feed back into future analysis.

Processing depth — why it's a decision at Gate 1

What a run/don't-run gate costs. A binary choice forces every signal into either a full 7-stage analysis or the bin. Most are worth retaining but not analyzing, and a binary gate discards exactly those.

  • A ladder, not a switch — archive (set aside) · note (insight recorded, S2) · structure (opportunity framed, S3) · evaluate (persona scoring, S4) · decide (full run, gated routing). Each depth stops where it already has something worth keeping, so retaining costs one stage rather than seven.
  • Chosen at Gate 1, not at intake — depth cannot be judged before classification. S1–S2 are cheap and produce exactly the inputs the choice needs; deciding earlier is guessing, deciding later pays for stages I may not want.

S4 · Multi-persona evaluation

Four viewpoints score the same opportunity in parallel, each isolated:

  • Explorer (how far can we go? — impact) · Strategist (does this belong? — fit) · Builder (can we build it? — feasibility) · Skeptic (what if we're wrong? — confidence).
  • Not generic AI roles — the PM's own evaluation perspectives made explicit. A bias-control mechanism, not multi-agent orchestration.
  • Why four axes, not one verdict — a blended score hides the split that decides routing: is a 3 a strong idea we cannot build, or an easy but mediocre one?
  • Why a Skeptic — the other three are constructive framings, which score high systematically.
  • Why isolated — a persona that sees another anchors on it, and convergence imitates consensus. The 12-point rubric scores Persona Independence.

S5 · Deterministic routing

composite = Impact·0.35 + Fit·0.30 + Feasibility·0.20 + Confidence·0.15
→ ≤1.5 Kill · ≥3.5 & confidence≥4 PRD · else PoC
  • Routing is code, not the LLM — eliminates cross-run/model routing variance; a decision record is only auditable if it reproduces.
  • Weights are per-product config — how much impact counts against feasibility is my judgment, and it differs by product. That belongs in a file I version, not in a prompt.
  • Assumptions are marked Blocking or Adjusting by one test — if this turns out false, is there still a version worth building? No → Blocking, and the run routes to a PoC to settle that question before anyone commits to a PRD.

Sensing — capture before mapping

  • OpenAI's July 2026 disclosure on Hugging Face model evaluation, relevant to one Knox product, never reached Gate 0 — found by hand, with the pipeline already running. The funnel rejected it 4 ways: no first-party source, product-shaped queries, product-derived keyword gate, product-gated scoring.
  • The principle: capture and mapping are separate decisions. Domain vocabulary admits an item on its own; the lanes then decide what costs a decision — submit costs one at Gate 0, capture-only costs none and stays promotable. Why — product mapping was the admission condition at all four stages, so importance was never judged independently of product fit.

Sensing — capture before mapping (cont.)

  • What a live run showed — half the vocabulary was dead. 33 of 70 keywords were written hyphenated (mobile-threat) and matched literally, so they never matched the spacing real articles use: "Mobile threat defense vendors compared" was rejected despite two keywords for it.
  • And 59 fresh candidates were lost in a single tick — when a tick ran out of room, "no room right now" was written into the same ledger that records "already seen", so those candidates were never looked at again. They are now simply deferred and compete again next tick.
  • Same feeds, before → after: 6 accepted · 59 burned → 12 accepted · 64 still eligible. Recall is now a tested property.

01 / 03The biggest lever isn't the model

  • Expected right-sizing the model to be the main win; the bigger lever was those two layers of determinism around the LLM.
  • Why the model wasn't it — my failure mode was never bad answers but inconsistent ones, and a stronger model gives better answers with the same variance.
  • Wins two things at once: cost ↓, consistency ↑ — maximizing consistency & evolvability, my top-2 quality attributes.
  • It's not "coding with AI" — it's building reliable scaffolding for AI. Harness engineering helps but is no substitute for structure.
Jack H. ParkStudio
19 / 24

02 / 03Context is still king; isolate it via separated agents

  • Keeping irrelevant context from mixing mattered most — the contamination is not extra data, it is an extra frame.
  • Best mechanism: split agents (Hermes profiles) — by identity / persona / R&R, not just data. Same agent with different files doesn't fix it: the frame travels with the agent.
  • "Agent" connotes autonomy, but specialization is the real point — not every agent needs autonomy. The value is in the constraints: avatar read-only, local confirms before it writes, scratch holds no production tokens.
  • As autonomy & parallelism rise, traceability & visibility become indispensable — with seven of the eight running concurrently, an unlogged action is unreconstructable.

03 / 03A green check is a claim, not evidence

Three failures in one week, same shape: the monitor was healthy, and the monitor was wrong.

  • A filter that matched nothing — the digest counted a status value the engine's enum never had, so the loop never ran; "✅ stale: 0" held seven weeks while nine waited, and the fixtures carried the same wrong value.
  • A lint looser than its consumers — the validator stripped each line before matching, so an indented block passed while strict parsers skipped it whole; nine files silently lost every field.
  • A job that kept succeeding — the weekly Notion export ran green four weeks while the corpus sat three weeks stale.

Takeaway 7 (cont.)
What the three had in common

  • The fix that generalizes: pin the check to the source of truth, not to a fixture. Traceability is not verification.
  • Why each check missed it — none measured the thing that mattered: a value the source never emits, a form the consumers reject, an exit code instead of the corpus's freshness.

Limits

  • Single operator, n=1. The 4-fixture harness tests consistency against my own past judgment, not correctness against the market.
  • Scoring weights are calibrated, not validated. They reflect my judgment; there is no external benchmark for them.
  • Closed-loop learning is early. The corpus accumulates, but not yet enough decision history to measurably improve later analysis.
  • The human is the throughput ceiling, by design. Current mitigations are the dashboard and scheduled reminders, not auto-advance.
  • Next: the engine already labels what it suggested against what I chose at each gate — but Gate 3 holds two such datapoints, so there is no agreement rate yet. Accumulating them gates any rise in autonomy.

What transfers to a team

The software doesn't transfer — a solo pipeline isn't a team process. What does:

  • Gates → scheduled decision reviews. A defined point where a person decides, with the available options enumerated in advance.
  • Rubric → evaluation criteria. Score the reasoning, not only the outcome, and rerun when it fails the threshold.
  • Wiki → decision record. Why an option was rejected, retrievable later by someone who was not present.
  • Dashboard → decision telemetry. Count of pending decisions, time waiting, and the stage where they stall.
  • A team's constraint is alignment, not throughput — what survives is whatever makes disagreement cheaper to resolve. Start with the decision record and the rubric: lowest setup cost, no new tool, and both give a disagreement something to point at.
